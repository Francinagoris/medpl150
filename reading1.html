Reading and Written Response #1

I use image making and photography to capture moments, spaces, and people as I see them. As most people do, when they take pictures when they travel or see something interesting or worth capturing. I like to take a lot of still shots where I don’t try to guide you to look at a specific person or thing, but instead you can just take the moment to look at the details of everything being shown to you. Therefore, as I am reading more of Susan Sontag’s “In Plato Cave”, I can agree with the idea that “Although there is a sense in which the camera does indeed capture reality, not just interpret it, photographs are as such an interpretation of the world as paintings a drawings are” (Sontag 6-7). When we take photos a lot of the times, we just want to capture a moment that is Instagram story worthy, and we are placing our cameras in a certain way so that you capture just what you want to get, so it's your own interpretation of that moment. Similarly to painting, you may have a model or an object in front of you and depending on what angle you paint it from what colors you choose, essentially you are painting your own version of that object or model. As a result, I can always try to capture my reality or a moment as I am experiencing it, but it will always be an interpretation of that moment from my perspective.
A lot of the time we take pictures as a way to solidify the realness or the reality that we are experiencing. When travelling the one thing we all do is take pictures of our time abroad or away from home. Those images are the proof of our lived experiences, therefore, the same way we use photography to capture our fun memories, our accomplishments and the small moment we can use it to make a statement. Make a statement of the reality that we are living in today, because you can’t deny a picture or a video. Although, in today’s day in age there is a lot of use of AI, still not everything is AI. The videos and pictures that have been captured of ICE killing innocent civilians and abusing their power to make us feel small and scared are statements. Once these moments are captured and shared with the world they in some way, as talked about by Susan Sontag, become “immortal”. Every time we capture something and share it with the world it makes it real, we know the name and faces of people who have been activists, who stood up against the wrongdoings of those in power and who have been important people in history because their images were taken and an image can’t be denied. Consequently, I believe photography solidifies reality and is extremely important because one image can become a statement of the truth.
